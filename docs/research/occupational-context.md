# Put job examples in context

A job posting describes one employer's requirements. Occupational statistics answer broader questions. This view uses **national May 2025 wages** and **2025–2035 projections** from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, reviewed in Chrome on **November 5, 2026**. O*NET pages confirm the occupation names and codes; their visible occupation updates are labeled 2026.

## Interpretation rules

- Broad occupation medians cover different industries and experience levels. They are not starting salaries, AI-specialist pay, or an estimate for NJIT graduates.
- Percentage growth is different from the number of openings, replacement hiring, or the chance of getting a job.
- The role-to-occupation relationships above are editorial crosswalks. AI product, governance, platform and research work may map to several occupations; no exact AI-title estimates are implied.
- National data do not establish NYC/NJ pay or remote availability. Location-specific wage analysis would require a separately verified OEWS series and geographic definition.
- Values are transcribed without inflation adjustments, annualizing internship rates, or mixing the small employer sample with national estimates.

## Retained data and review

The repository retains the values, URLs, review date, release description, units, periods, occupation codes, mapping notes and transformation method in [occupational-context.json](https://github.com/kaw393939/ai-career-research/blob/main/docs/research/occupational-context.json).
